Hiring a freelance web designer in Bristol can get you a site that's every bit as good as an agency's, often for a fraction of the price — but only if you pick the right person. The gap between a great freelancer and a costly mistake usually comes down to a handful of questions asked (or not asked) before any money changes hands. Here's exactly what to look for.

Freelancer or agency — which do you need?

A freelancer is typically cheaper, more personal and quicker to deal with, because you talk to the person doing the work. An agency brings more capacity and cover for large or ongoing projects. For most small business sites in Bristol, a good freelancer is the sweet spot: agency-level quality without agency overheads. If your project is big, complex, or needs guaranteed cover, lean towards an agency.

What a freelance web designer in Bristol charges

Expect roughly £25–£75 an hour, or a fixed project price of £800–£5,000 depending on scope. For a defined project, a fixed quote is usually safer than hourly — you know the total up front and the designer carries the risk of overruns. Nine questions to ask before you hire

  1. Can I see live sites you've built? Screenshots can be mock-ups. Ask for real URLs and visit them on your phone.
  2. Have you worked with businesses like mine? Relevant experience beats a big-name portfolio.
  3. What's included in the price — and what isn't? Copywriting, images, SEO and revisions are common hidden extras.
  4. Who writes the content? If it's you, factor in the time; if it's them, check it's in the quote.
  5. What's the timeline? A clear, realistic schedule is a sign of someone who's done this before.
  6. How many rounds of changes do I get? "Unlimited revisions" often means a vague scope; a defined number is healthier for both sides.
  7. Will the site be mobile-friendly and fast? It should go without saying in 2026 — make sure they say it anyway.
  8. What happens after launch? Who handles updates, hosting and problems, and what does support cost?
  9. Do I own everything at the end? Domain, hosting, files and content should be yours on final payment.

Red flags to walk away from

  • No portfolio, or only screenshots. You need to see and click real, live work.
  • A price that's dramatically lower than everyone else. Something is usually missing — often SEO, mobile testing, or ownership.
  • Vague answers about who owns the site. If they dodge this, assume the worst.
  • Pressure to pay 100% up front. A staged deposit is normal; full payment before you've seen anything is not.
  • Poor communication during the sales stage. It rarely improves once they've got the deposit.

Make sure you own your website

This is where small businesses get burned. Protect yourself by agreeing, in writing, that on final payment you own:

  • The domain name — registered in your name and account, not the designer's.
  • The hosting account — or at least full admin access to it.
  • The design files and content.
  • Admin logins to the site itself.

Get this in the contract before you pay a deposit. A trustworthy freelancer won't blink at the request — it's a completely standard thing to ask.

Protect yourself with a simple agreement

You don't need a solicitor for a small project, but you do need something in writing that covers scope, price, payment stages, timeline, number of revisions and ownership. A short, clear agreement prevents almost every common dispute — and again, a good freelancer will welcome it, because it protects them too.

The bottom line

A skilled freelance web designer in Bristol can be the best value in the market for a small business site. Do your due diligence, ask the questions above, get the important things in writing, and you'll get agency-quality work with the personal service — and price — that only a freelancer can offer.
