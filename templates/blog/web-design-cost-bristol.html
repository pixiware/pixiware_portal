If you've started ringing round for quotes, you've probably noticed the same thing everyone does: web design costs in Bristol seem to be all over the place. One designer quotes £600, the next quotes £6,000, and they're both describing "a website". This guide breaks down what you actually get at each price point in 2026, and the six things that move the number up or down — so you can tell a fair quote from a bad one.

What changes the price of a website?

Two quotes for "a small business website" can differ by thousands of pounds for good reasons. These are the six factors that move the number most.

1. Number of pages and content

A five-page site is quicker to design and build than a twenty-page one. More importantly, who writes the words matters. If you supply the copy, you save money; if the designer writes it for you, that's professional copywriting time on top.

2. Custom design vs template

A site built on a tidied-up template is faster and cheaper. A design created from scratch around your brand costs more but looks like nobody else's. For most small businesses in Bristol, a lightly customised, well-chosen template is the sensible middle ground.

3. Features and functionality

Contact forms and a Google map are standard. Online booking, payments, membership logins, multi-language, or integrations with your CRM all add development time — and cost.

4. SEO and getting found

A site that's merely "live" is different from a site that's built to rank. Proper page structure, fast loading, mobile optimisation and local SEO groundwork take extra effort but are what actually bring in Bristol customers. See our guide to small business web design in Bristol for what that involves.

5. Who builds it — freelancer or agency

A skilled freelance web designer in Bristol usually costs less than an agency, because there's less overhead. An agency brings a bigger team and more cover for larger projects. Neither is automatically "better value" — it depends on the size of the job.

6. Ongoing costs

The build is a one-off; running the site is not. Budget for these:

  • Domain name: £10–£20 a year.
  • Hosting: £5–£30 a month for a small business site.
  • Maintenance / support: optional care plans commonly £15–£50 a month for updates, backups and security.

Is a cheap website ever a false economy?

Sometimes a £500 site is exactly right — a new sole trader who just needs to exist online. But if that site loads slowly, isn't mobile-friendly, or can't be found on Google, it quietly costs you enquiries every month. Many owners who start with the cheapest option end up paying a second designer within 18 months to rebuild it properly. Doing it once, well, is often the cheaper route overall.

How to get an accurate quote

The clearer your brief, the more accurate — and comparable — your quotes. Before you ask a Bristol designer for a price, be ready to answer:

  • What does the website need to do — get calls, take bookings, sell products?
  • Roughly how many pages, and do you have the content?
  • Do you have a logo and brand, or do you need those too?
  • Any must-have features (booking, payments, a specific integration)?
  • What's your realistic budget range and deadline?

Give three designers the same brief and the quotes suddenly become easy to compare. If one is dramatically cheaper, ask what's not included — that's usually where the difference hides.
